It also covers what happens when you open one of my pages.

4. The application form

What I receive. Your answers to the form's questions: your business and the size of your team, the tools you use, the work that slows you down, the earliest date you could start, whether you approve and pay for the purchase, and the statements you tick. Your name, your email address, and your website or main social profile. If you arrived from one of my messages, the link you used. If I decide to accept you, I then ask you by email for your business's legal and trading names, its address, the country where it is established, its tax number, and who will sign or pay, for the sanctions check and, if you go ahead, the invoice. If you name someone else who will sign or pay, I use their name and contact details for the same check and, if you go ahead, the invoice; I got them from you.

Why I use it, and the legal basis.

  • To review your application, reply to you and, if I accept you, prepare your setup. The legal basis is the steps you ask for before a contract.
  • To keep a record of who applied. The legal basis is my legitimate interest in that record.
  • Before I send anyone a payment link, I also check where their business is established, and their name and their business's name against the EU and US sanctions lists. The legal basis is my legitimate interest in not dealing with anyone my services terms exclude for sanctions reasons.
  • If you go ahead, to invoice you. The legal basis is my legal duty to issue and keep invoices.

Who holds it.

  • Tally (Tally BV, Belgium) runs the form and stores your answers in the EU for me.
  • A copy goes to my own application inbox, a server hosted by Hetzner in the EU.
  • My replies go by email, and my email provider may store them outside the EU under an EU adequacy decision or the EU standard contractual clauses, a copy of which I send on request.
  • If you go ahead, my accountant receives your invoices.
  • I do not give your application answers to any AI service.

How long I keep it. If I do not accept you, or you do not go ahead, I delete your answers within 90 days of my last reply. If you become a client, I delete them within 90 days after our engagement ends. Tally says it then removes deleted answers from its backups within 90 days. The result of a sanctions check is kept with the acceptance record, and for anyone I do not accept it is deleted on the same 90-day rule. If I do not accept you, or you do not go ahead, I delete the business details I asked for by email with your application, within 90 days of my last reply. If you become a client, I keep them, with your invoices and contract records, for as long as tax and accounting law requires, as my services terms say (clause 12.4).

Do you have to give it? Your answers are needed for me to consider your application. Without them I cannot. The business details I ask for by email are needed before I can send an acceptance and a payment link.

Decisions. I read every application myself. Nothing is decided automatically.

5. The Bottleneck Map application

What I receive. Your answers to the map form: what you sell and to whom, what a new client is worth to you, where your enquiries come from and how many arrived last week, how fast you reply and how often you follow up, your calls or proposals and how many became clients, your six planning numbers or that you do not know them, how many clients you serve and the most you can serve, what you posted and where, your weekly hours on content, enquiries and calls, the tools you use, and the part you would hand over first, and why. Your name, your email address, and your business name or main social profile. If you came from my calculator, the numbers you typed there arrive with your answers.

Why I use it, and the legal basis. To prepare your map and send it to you, which is what you ask for when you apply. The legal basis is providing the map you ask for.

How the map is made. A program I run on my own servers reads your answers and drafts the map. No AI service sees your answers or the draft. I read every draft, write my own read into it and send it myself. Nothing about you is decided automatically.

Who holds it. Tally (Tally BV, Belgium) runs the form and stores your answers in the EU for me. A copy goes to my own map inbox, a server hosted by Hetzner in the EU, and the draft is made on another server of mine hosted by Hetzner in the EU. I send the map by email, and my email provider may store it outside the EU under an EU adequacy decision or the EU standard contractual clauses, a copy of which I send on request. I do not give your answers to any AI service.

How long I keep it. Within 90 days of sending your map, I delete your answers, in Tally and in my map inbox, and my copies of the map, including the email I sent. Tally says it then removes deleted answers from its backups within 90 days.

Your email address. I use it only to send your map: one email, no list, and nothing after it unless you write back.

Do you have to give it? Your answers are needed to make your map. Without them I cannot make it.

6. My Instagram messages

What happens. If you comment a word on one of my Instagram posts, or send me that word in a message, an automatic reply sends you the link you asked for: my application page or one of my free guides. For the application page it sends one reminder about 20 hours later. ManyChat runs those replies for me. Its data processing terms name the company as Manychat, Inc., its subsidiaries or affiliates.

My AI assistant. If you send me any other message there, my AI assistant may answer it. Its first reply in our conversation says it is the HaldenBuilds AI assistant. It answers questions about my work, and it can pass our conversation to me. I can read our conversation and reply myself at any time, and once I do, the assistant stays out of it until I hand it back. If you ask it to stop messaging you, it stops.

How it writes a reply. ManyChat passes your message to my own server, hosted by Hetzner in the EU, where the assistant runs. To write each reply, my server sends the words of our recent messages, with my fixed instructions and information about my business, to OpenRouter (OpenRouter, Inc., in the United States). OpenRouter passes them to OpenAI, in the United States, whose AI model writes a draft. My server does not send OpenRouter your Instagram name or account. A program on my server checks each draft before anything is sent.

What ManyChat holds for me. Your Instagram account as Instagram shares it with ManyChat, the messages we exchange there, and labels saying which word you used, whether you asked for the link, whether it was sent and whether I have taken over our conversation. I do not ask for your email address there.

What my server keeps. The id ManyChat gives your contact, when each message came, what the assistant decided and the words of each reply it wrote. The words of your own messages are wiped after 7 days, except that when the assistant passes our conversation to me, it keeps your question and what it wrote, for me to read.

What OpenRouter and OpenAI keep. OpenRouter does not store the words or the reply under the settings I use. It stores details of each request, such as its size and timing, without the content. It also sorts a small sample of requests into topic categories for its own statistics, using an AI model with a zero data retention policy, and says it stores that categorisation anonymously. OpenRouter lists OpenAI as keeping what it receives, without saying for how long, and as not training its models on it. OpenRouter does not train AI models on it either.

Why I use it, and the legal basis. To send you the link you asked for, and to answer the messages you send me. The legal basis is my legitimate interest in answering your requests and your messages.

How long I keep it. I delete your contact in ManyChat, with its labels, within 90 days of our last message there. If you apply, it is deleted on the rules in section 4 or 7 instead. I delete what my server keeps about you within 90 days of our last message, except a note that you asked the assistant to stop, which holds no message and which I keep while the assistant runs, so that it stays stopped. To have any of it deleted sooner, email support@haldenbuilds.com.

Who else. Instagram (Meta) carries the messages under its own privacy policy. ManyChat's data processing terms say its production databases and processing servers are in an Amazon Web Services data centre in Frankfurt, in the EU, and that it may also process the data outside the European Economic Area, including in the United States. They say such transfers are subject to the Data Privacy Framework or, where it does not apply, to the EU standard contractual clauses (Commission Implementing Decision (EU) 2021/914), a copy of which I send on request. Apart from the assistant's use of OpenRouter and OpenAI, I do not give these messages to any AI service.

7. The free public setups

Each month I set up my system free, in public, for up to two businesses. This section covers the application form for those setups and, if you are picked, the publishing consent you sign.

What I receive. Your answers to the application: your name, your business name and your email; what kind of business you run and how many people are on the team; what you sell and to whom; where enquiries reach you and about how many; where and how often you post; the tools you use; the work that slows you down; which of your numbers you can read today; whether you decide yourself; the month you could start; the statements you tick; whether you would sign the publishing consent; and, if you tell me, how you heard about me. If you are picked: the signed consent, the before and after numbers you tick on it, read from your own accounts, and what you give me to publish, such as your words, your video and screens of your setup.

Why I use it, and the legal basis.

  • To pick who gets a free setup and to reply to you. The legal basis is the steps you ask for before an agreement.
  • If you are picked, to set it up for you. The legal basis is our agreement: the setup, free, in return for the publishing you agree to.
  • To publish what you tick, and only after your yes on each piece. The legal basis is your consent. You can withdraw it at any time, all of it or only some of it, as the consent form says. Withdrawing does not affect what was lawfully published before it.
  • To keep a record of who applied and of what you agreed to. The legal basis is my legitimate interest in that record and in being able to show your consent.

How picks are made. A program I run checks each application against the conditions on the form and scores it from your answers and ticks. An application that fails a condition, for example enquiries that carry health information, is not picked. I read the applications with the highest scores and pick myself. No AI service sees your answers. You can ask me to read yours myself.

Who holds it.

  • Tally (Tally BV, Belgium) runs the application form and stores your answers in the EU for me.
  • A copy goes to my own application inbox, a server hosted by Hetzner in the EU. If you are picked, your signed consent and the numbers read from your accounts are kept there with your application.
  • My replies go by email, and my email provider may store them outside the EU, as section 4 says.
  • What I publish, anyone can see. I publish it on my own accounts: my Instagram account and my pages, which GitHub hosts. Instagram (Meta) and GitHub show it under their own privacy policies.

How long I keep it. If you are not picked, I delete your application within 90 days of telling you. If you are picked, I keep your application until 90 days after your setup ends, and I keep the signed consent and the numbers read from your accounts for as long as anything about your business is published, and for 90 days after it comes down. Tally says it then removes deleted answers from its backups within 90 days. The score sheet I use to pick holds an application number, the checks and the scores, none of your answers, and I delete it on the same rules.

If you withdraw. Within 7 days I take down every post on my own accounts that shows what you withdrew. I cannot recall copies others have already shared or saved, but I ask those I know of to take theirs down.

Do you have to give it? Your answers are needed for me to consider your application. To be picked, you sign the publishing consent. Which parts you tick on it is your choice, and naming your business is optional: it only counts in your favour when I pick.
